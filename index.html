How to Use the Grade Calculator

The Grade Calculator helps students compute their current course grade from graded assignments, determine what they need on the final exam to reach a target grade, and calculate weighted category grades for courses with different assessment weights.

Add your completed assignments with their scores and weights (or use the category-based approach for courses with multiple assignment types). The calculator shows your current average, the projected final grade, and — using the final exam weight — the minimum score needed on the final to achieve your target.

A key nuance is the difference between weighted and unweighted grading. In weighted grading, different categories (exams, homework, participation) contribute different percentages to the final grade. A 90% average in homework worth 20% of the grade matters less than a 70% in exams worth 50%. Always know your syllabus's grade breakdown.

📊 Worked Example

Course with 40% exams, 30% homework, 20% projects, 10% participation:

  • Exams (85% avg × 40%): 34.0 points
  • Homework (95% avg × 30%): 28.5 points
  • Projects (78% avg × 20%): 15.6 points
  • Participation (90% × 10%): 9.0 points
  • Current grade: 87.1% (B+)

Common Use Cases

  • ✅ Calculating your current grade before final exams

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my final grade from a weighted syllabus?

Multiply each category average by its weight (as a decimal), then sum all the results. Example: if homework (0.30) = 90%, exams (0.50) = 80%, and participation (0.20) = 100%: (0.30×90) + (0.50×80) + (0.20×100) = 27 + 40 + 20 = 87% final grade.

What grade do I need on the final exam?

If you know your current grade and the final exam weight: Required Final Score = (Target Grade − Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight)) ÷ Final Weight. Example: need 90%, currently at 85%, final worth 30%: (90 − 85×0.70) ÷ 0.30 = (90 − 59.5) ÷ 0.30 = 101.7% — meaning a 90% is unachievable even with 100% on the final.

What is grade rounding and does it matter?

Some instructors round up from X.5 (e.g. 89.5 rounds to 90 = A). Others use strict cutoffs (89.9 = B+). Always know your professor's rounding policy before finals, as it could mean the difference between grades. Some instructors 'bump' borderline students based on participation or improvement trends.

How does extra credit affect my grade?

Extra credit adds points to your grade above 100%, or adds a fixed number to your average. If your class average is 85% and extra credit adds 3 points, your new average is 88%. If extra credit is offered on individual assignments, calculate the new score as: (Points earned + Extra credit) ÷ Total possible × 100.

What GPA does each letter grade correspond to?

Standard US letter grade to GPA conversion: A+/A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, D− = 0.7, F = 0.0. Percentage cutoffs vary by institution but typically A = 90–100%, B = 80–89%, C = 70–79%, D = 60–69%, F = below 60%.
